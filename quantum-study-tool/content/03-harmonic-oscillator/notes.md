# The Quantum Harmonic Oscillator

Almost any potential looks like a parabola near a stable minimum, so the
harmonic oscillator is the single most useful model in physics — from molecular
vibrations to the quantized modes of a field. Like the infinite square well it
can be solved exactly, but its ladder of evenly spaced levels and its non-trivial
eigenfunctions make it far richer.

## The potential

A particle of mass $m$ in a quadratic potential

$$
V(x) = \tfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2
$$

obeys the time-independent Schrödinger equation

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \tfrac{1}{2} m \omega^2 x^2\,\psi = E\,\psi .
$$

## Two ways to solve it

**Algebraically (ladder operators).** Define

$$
a_\pm = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 m \hbar \omega}}\left(\mp i \hat{p} + m \omega \hat{x}\right),
$$

so that $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\!\left(a_+ a_- + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)$. These operators
raise and lower the energy by one quantum: if $\psi_n$ has energy $E_n$, then
$a_+\psi_n \propto \psi_{n+1}$ and $a_-\psi_n \propto \psi_{n-1}$. A lowest state
must exist with $a_-\psi_0 = 0$, and the entire spectrum is generated by applying
$a_+$ repeatedly.

**Analytically (Hermite polynomials).** Solving the differential equation
directly — after stripping off the Gaussian asymptotic behavior — yields the same
states in closed form (below).

## The stationary states

With the natural length scale $\xi = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x$,

$$
\boxed{\,\psi_n(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n\,n!}}\,H_n(\xi)\,e^{-\xi^2/2}\,}
\qquad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots
$$

with **evenly spaced** energies

$$
\boxed{\,E_n = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega\,.}
$$

The $H_n$ are the (physicists') Hermite polynomials,

$$
H_0 = 1, \quad H_1 = 2\xi, \quad H_2 = 4\xi^2 - 2, \quad H_3 = 8\xi^3 - 12\xi, \ \dots
$$

A few things to notice:

- The levels form a uniform ladder, $\Delta E = \hbar\omega$ for every step —
  unlike the well's $n^2$ spacing.
- The **ground state** $n = 0$ carries the irreducible zero-point energy
  $E_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}\hbar\omega > 0$.
- $\psi_n$ has definite **parity** $(-1)^n$ and exactly $n$ nodes.
- The states are orthonormal: $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_m^*\,\psi_n\,dx = \delta_{mn}$.
